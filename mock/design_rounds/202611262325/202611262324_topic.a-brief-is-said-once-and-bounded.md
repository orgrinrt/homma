# a-brief-is-said-once-and-bounded

A review of the brief round, fixed before it lands.

## What the review found

- A brief row says a brief on every prompt whose text differs from the last one, whatever its
  `ttl`, because a new text counts as a new work. With the summarising pass off, which is the
  default, that is up to twelve candidates with their summaries on every message. A brief is
  meant to come before a unit of work and again on a re-task or on demand, so that less sits in
  the context, not more with every message.
- Nothing bounds what a brief prints. `--limit` caps the things named, but neither how many
  reasons or quotes a thing carries nor how long the whole is.
- A placed line of the answer with no quote becomes a reason, so the model's paraphrase of a
  rule's body is printed unchecked, and a thing can carry several reasons.
- A quote is taken where it is any substring of the lines named, so a fragment as short as a
  word passes, and one that leaves out a "never" reverses what it quotes.
- Where the brief cannot be made, the line that says so comes on every new prompt and says why
  but not what to do.

## The fix

- A brief row is due as any inform row is: once in a session, and again once `ttl` has passed,
  whatever the text. A prompt inside the ttl is not briefed even when its text is new. The
  re-ask is the verb: `homma what --brief -` with the work on stdin gives the brief at any time.
  The record keeps no digest of the text briefed, so `briefed` goes from the record.
- A prompt with an empty text, or one nothing bears on, says nothing and is not recorded, so the
  first prompt that something does bear on still gets its brief.
- One reason a thing, taken only from the line the answer places at the thing's heading. A
  placed line under a heading that carries no quote is not a reason; it is left out and counted
  with the dropped lines.
- A quote is taken only where it is a whole line of its source, or the whole of the lines it
  names, compared with whitespace folded and with a line's leading list, heading or quote marks
  and its emphasis marks set aside on both sides. A fragment is dropped and counted.
- At most three quotes a thing; the rest are counted as dropped.
- The rendered brief is at most 3000 bytes. Things are kept best first while the whole fits;
  the rest are counted, and the brief says how many were left out and that `homma what --brief`
  with a smaller `--limit` or `homma what <words>` names them.
- A brief that cannot be made says so once a ttl like any other text of the row, with the
  reason and the command that makes it by hand.

## Tests

- A second, different prompt inside the ttl is not briefed; one after it is.
- A prompt nothing bears on, then one something does, inside the ttl: the second is briefed.
- One reason a thing, from the heading line; a placed line without a quote is dropped.
- A fragment of a line is dropped; the whole line, with its list mark left off, is taken.
- A fourth quote of a thing is dropped and counted.
- A brief over the byte cap keeps the best things under it and says how many were left out.
- The failure line names the command.
