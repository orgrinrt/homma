# a-brief-of-what-bears-on-a-unit-of-work

## What is wanted

Before a unit of work starts, the agent doing it is given a short brief of what
bears on that work: the passages of the rules that apply, the skills worth
loading and the tools worth using, chosen from the work's own text by the
summarising pass. The cards of the rules stay always loaded; the brief carries
the elaborations, and only the parts that matter to the work at hand.

- The brief is extractive. Each rule, skill or tool is named, with its own lines
  quoted and one line on why it applies, since a paraphrase of a rule changes
  it, and every quoted span is checked to exist in its source.
- Candidates are picked first, so the call reads only those and stays under its
  budget.
- It is kept on the digests of the work's text and of what it read, asked again
  for a new text or on demand.
- Which things it surfaced is logged, so its misses can be measured later.
- It is generic over what a workspace calls its rules and skills.

## Readings taken

- A verb a hook calls, not a hook of its own. `homma what` already answers which
  tool, script, verb, skill or rule bears on what an agent wants to do, from the
  sources where they sit. The brief is the same question asked with a whole
  work's text in place of a few words, so it is `homma what --brief <file>`, one
  mode of the one verb, and not a second entry point.
- The candidates are `what`'s own ranking. A query of a few words needs a share
  of its words matched; a work's text has hundreds, and one thing answers a few
  of them, so for a brief that share is replaced by a floor of two words, and
  the floor of a fifth of the best score stays. The mockspace commands are left
  out, since one is a line of a listing with nothing to quote.
- The pass is the summarising pass, with the candidates' text as its content and
  a brief asking for the lines that apply. Its rule that every line names its
  place is what the span check reads: each quoted span must be in the content
  line its place names, and a line whose span is not there is dropped and
  counted. The places are then given back as the thing they belong to.
- The hook is a nudge row. One framework holds what a hook says before a call or
  a prompt, so the brief is an `inform` row with `brief = true`: on
  `UserPromptSubmit` it says the brief of the prompt, once for each prompt text
  in a session. `matches` and `when` narrow which prompts are briefed, as for
  any row. No second hook table, no wrapper of its own.
- Kept as the ask keeps its answers, by the same cache, under `homma/brief/` in
  the workspace's git directory, with the expiry and the cap under `[ask]`.
- Where the pass is off or fails, the brief is the candidates themselves, each
  with its command and summary, and one line saying why there is no extract. An
  agent still learns what to open, and no call is paid.

## Not here

- Taking the skill listing and the tool list out of a session's always-loaded
  context is the workspace's configuration, once the brief is in use.
- Asking by meaning waits on an embedder in the index crate; the candidates are
  by words until then.
