# The guard nothing tested, and the comparison that lets forks through

**Phase:** TOPIC
**Scope:** `homma-api`, `homma-core`, `homma-org`, `homma`

The previous round was convened to eliminate fixes that were assertions with no
measurement behind them. **It shipped its own headline fix as one.**

Deleting the entire remote check in `provision` leaves the suite at 259 passing,
zero failing. Nothing anywhere constructs `ProvisionError::WrongRemote`; the only
test touching that path uses a fake whose `origin_url` returns the matching URL
unconditionally, so it cannot enter the refusing arm. The round's own audit
checked four mechanisms by mutation and skipped the one the round was named for.

That is worth stating plainly rather than filing as an oversight: **the audit
covered what was easy to mutate, and the thing it was for went unchecked.** A
mutation audit that picks its own targets is a coverage claim by the author about
the author's work.

## And the comparison underneath it is wrong

`same_repo` compares the last path segment, so any two repositories sharing a name
are the same repository as far as the guard is concerned. Reproduced: a root
cloned from `ownerA/content` and a workspace already cloned from `ownerB/content`
passes the check, prints "already there" and exits 0, leaving the Hand holding the
wrong repository permanently.

**For a fork or a mirror this is the expected case, not an exotic one.** The
workspace has a public repository and a private one, and cloning shapes differ
between hosts, so names collide by design.

## The rest, in order of what they cost

**A symlinked registry is clobbered and the write is lost.** Write-beside-and-
rename replaces the symlink with a regular file: the entry lands on the link, the
real file never sees it, and the operator's registry is silently stale with a
divergent copy beside it. Worse, the previous round added a comment above that
line telling the next reader not to touch it, which makes the defect harder to
find rather than easier.

**The fix for the vacuous global-config comparison is itself vacuous.**
`/etc/gitconfig` is pushed unconditionally, so the non-empty assertion cannot
fail, and that file does not exist on this machine: the test compares `[None]` to
`[None]` and reports ok under a stripped environment. What was needed is that at
least one of those paths **exists**.

**`local` initialises a repository inside another repository's working tree.**
`is_repo` tests for a `.git` beside the path and never looks upward, so a registry
in a subdirectory of a checked-out repository gets its own `.git`, and the
directories and definitions land in that repository's tree. Deny item 2 forbids
exactly that.

**A blind global replace corrupted the comment explaining the rename it was part
of.** It now reads "Named staffing rather than staffing because staffing already
means what a reference derives", three words after saying a vocabulary crate using
one word for two concepts has failed at its only job. A second comment lost the
word *standing* in its other sense, where it meant resident.

**The file-size limit moved rather than being respected**, again: `org.rs` went
from 703 to 238 by moving the overflow into a 536-line file, and `cli_smoke.rs`
crossed the limit inside the same change.

## What this round does not pretend

Three of the four mutation counts in the previous round's own summary were wrong,
and it claimed no warnings while introducing one. Those are recorded here rather
than corrected in place, because the summary is a historical claim and the record
of it being wrong is worth more than a tidy number.
