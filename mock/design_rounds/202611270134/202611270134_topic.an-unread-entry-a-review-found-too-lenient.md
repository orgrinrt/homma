# An unread entry a review found too lenient

Round 202611270005 took an entry of the top level or of `[agent]` as unread when this engine reads no key under it. A review found three ways that is too lenient, and two places where the round's words did not hold.

## What a review found

1. `[repos]` and `[local]` are now taken as unread, but neither is a newer engine's feature. `[repos]` is retired with its per-repository settings, and `[local]` belongs to the local file alone. Before the round both were refused, and the tests holding that were rewritten to conform. The advice printed for them, to move the pin to a newer engine, is wrong too: no engine reads either.
2. A misspelt entry passes with only a status line. The misspelling is the whole entry: `[defualts]`, `homma_brnach`, `[agent.nudge]`.
3. Where an entry is taken out and the rest still refuses, the second parse runs on a rebuilt value and the refusal loses its line.
4. Two tests are weaker than they look: an assertion that cannot fail, and a `regen` control that never shows regen going on.

## Taken

- A named set is refused whatever the key probe says: `repos` and `local` at the top level. The two original tests come back as they were, beside the new ones.
- An entry one edit away from a name this engine reads in the same table is refused as a misspelling. One edit is one character inserted, removed, replaced, or two neighbours swapped, which covers all three probes. It is checked against the names of that table: the top level against the top-level names `keys` lists, `[agent]` against the names under it.
- The second parse reads the manifest's own text with the lines of each unread entry blanked, so a refusal of the rest keeps its line. Where the blanking misses an entry's shape, a multi-line value the line reading does not follow, the rebuilt value is parsed as before.
- The words are said once on stderr by every verb that loads the manifest, besides `homma status` and the refusing `agent regen`. `homma status` says them in its report and not again on stderr. The `[paths]` round ruled this out for unknown keys of a known table; an unread entry is a different case, since a misspelt table name is the whole entry.
- The assertion that cannot fail goes. The `regen` control runs with one stage on and an unread top-level entry, and regen goes on.

Options weighed for the misspelling:
- Refusing every unread entry and keeping the leniency for nothing. Refused: that undoes the ticket.
- A list of known future names. Refused: an engine cannot know a later one's.
- One edit from a known name (taken). The probe's names are the comparison, so no list is kept beside the types.
