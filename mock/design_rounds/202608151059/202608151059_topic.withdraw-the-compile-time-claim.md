# Withdraw the compile-time claim, and close the four mechanical findings

The seventh review of PR #26 returned MERGE AFTER FIXES with twenty of
twenty-five mutations caught, and **all twenty-one on the production side**. The
five survivors are test quality and documentation, and one of them is not a
repair.

## The one that is a decision rather than a fix

Four rounds have tried to make `every_field_is_classified_as_free_form_or_not`
close at compile time, and the mechanism cannot. `..` and `field: _` are always
available in a Rust pattern and no attribute reaches them, so a field added,
bound as `field: _`, and given `None` in the literal leaves the suite green with
zero warnings.

The sharpest part of the finding is that the guard's own error routes a reader
into the escape. `E0027` is what fires when a field is unnamed, and rustc's three
suggested remedies are, verbatim, `, forge_account }`, `, forge_account: _ }` and
`, .. }`. **Two of the three silently disarm the guard.**

And the round written to stop overstating the claim strengthened it: the previous
text said "There are no `_` patterns", which at least named the precondition it
depended on, and the correction deleted that clause.

So the claim is withdrawn rather than repaired. The destructure stays, demoted to
what it is: an aid that makes omitting a field noisy for anybody writing the
obvious thing. **The guarantee that actually covers an unknown field is
`unsafe_strings` deriving its list from the serialised form**, which sees every
field including the ones nobody has added yet, and needs no author to remember
anything.

This is the shape `conceding-is-an-answer-and-expert-code-is-a-spike.md`
describes from the other side: the useful output here is that the route is
closed, not a fifth attempt at it.

## The four that are fixes

**A sampled law.** Narrowing the walker's `is_control()` to `== '\n'` leaves the
suite green, because every hostile value in the suite is `\n`. The property is
asserted at one of the sixty-five characters `char::is_control` covers. `\x1b`
matters on its own, because `stand.rs:96` interpolates these into terminal
output.

**Two more instances of the singular-identity class**, in `//!` and `///`
comments rather than in markdown. Every candidate across three rounds was found
by grepping `*.md` and `*.tmpl`, and a doc comment is a rendered surface too.
The re-grep by property rather than by extension is the lesson and is in the src
changelist.

**An unpinned double.** `fake_git.rs:93` can substitute the author's values for
the committer and the suite stays green. Its comment says the committer is
asserted in `homma-org`, and `homma-org` has its own separate `FakeGit`, so this
double's committer path is asserted nowhere. It is invisible because
`stand.rs:363`'s fixture carries no committer, which quietly turns the four-field
assertion at `:365` into a two-field one: setup that helps, exactly as
`the-test-gate.md` names it.

**The PR body**, which carries the withdrawn claim and one further sentence
describing the present text rather than an enforced property.

## What is deliberately not changed

`DESIGN.md.tmpl:61` and `:114` say a Hand needs "a git identity" where a
consultant needs none. That is a claim about whether an entry carries one at all,
not about the lifecycle setting it, and rewriting it to name two identities would
make a sentence about presence read as a sentence about mechanism.
