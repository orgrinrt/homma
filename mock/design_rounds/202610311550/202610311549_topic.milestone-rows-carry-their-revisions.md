# Milestone rows carry their revisions

Op, 31 October 2026, verbatim: "Aye, we also need clear revs stored for the epochs/milestones so we need not guess in future"

The intent is the whole sentence: every milestone, and the epoch of work it closes, is recorded with exact commit revisions, so a later session never infers or asks for a base again. The wording "epochs/milestones" is kept for provenance and names no second kind of record.

## What was found

A row in `.homma/milestones.toml` holds `sha` and `at`. The milestone on kaski's `dev` is one row whose sha op had to pick by hand because nothing recorded it. The freeze, `msg <ch> freeze`, records a time and no revision, and the review pins its head by `git rev-parse origin/dev` and writes it nowhere. So three of the four revisions a milestone has, the base, the freeze and the head, live in nobody's record.

## What is settled

A row carries, by name: `n`, `base`, `freeze`, `freeze_at`, `head`, `tip` and `at`. `base` is the previous row's `tip`, absent on the first row where nothing earlier is recorded. `freeze` is the trunk's sha when the freeze began and `freeze_at` is that commit's committer date, so no clock is read. `head` is the sha the review pinned. `tip` is the trunk's sha at close, which the next row takes as `base`, and `at` is the date of that commit.

The epoch a milestone closes is the range `base..freeze` and the review covers `base..head`. Both are derived from the row and neither is stored, since a second copy of a range is a second place for it to be wrong.

The row is written in steps as the phases pass. `freeze` opens row `n` with `base` and `freeze`. `pin` sets `head`. `mark` sets `tip` and `at`. Each refuses a sha the trunk does not contain and a step taken out of order: a freeze while the last row is still open, a pin with no open row or one already pinned, a mark before the pin. A pinned head must contain the freeze, and a tip must contain the head.

A row of the old shape, `sha` and `at` only, stays readable and its `sha` is its `tip`. `mark` with no open row appends a row with `n`, `tip` and `at` and no freeze or head, which is the old call in the new shape. `base` prints the last row that has a tip, so while a milestone is open it prints the sha that milestone began from.

`show` prints the rows with their two ranges, and takes a number to print one. It reads the record as the trunk commits it, as `base` does.

## Options this chose among

Where the open row lives: in the same `.homma/milestones.toml` as a half-filled row, or in a second file for the milestone in progress. One file is kept, since one record is the point of the intent and a second file is a second place to lose a revision.

Where the ranges live: stored on the row, or derived. Derived, for the reason above.

How an old row is read: migrated by rewriting it, or read as it is. Read as it is, so no tool edits recorded history. The kaski row is numbered by hand once, in the job that lands this.
